3. Director of Studies (DoS)
=================
The person responsible for the school's examination and academic coordination, especially around examination administration and analysis.

1. Examination Management

The DoS is in charge of examination activities.

This includes:

Creating/managing examinations.
Setting examination dates and schedules.
Coordinating examination periods.
Monitoring whether examinations are progressing properly.
Managing examination-related activities across grades.

2. Invigilator Allocation

This was one of the specific roles you mentioned.

During examinations, the DoS allocates teachers as invigilators.

The system should																																		 make this simple rather than manually assigning every teacher one by one.

A sensible workflow would be:

Select Examination
        ↓
Select Date / Session
        ↓
System shows classes requiring invigilators
        ↓
System suggests eligible teachers
        ↓
DoS reviews/adjusts assignments
        ↓
Publish allocation

The system should prevent obvious conflicts, such as assigning a teacher to two examination rooms at the same time.

3. Marking Allocation

An important distinction here.

The teachers who teach a particular subject are the ones who should mark that subject.

For example:

English Examination
        ↓
System identifies English teachers
        ↓
DoS allocates eligible English teachers
        ↓
Teachers mark English

So the DoS shouldn't have to search through every teacher manually.

The system should filter teachers based on their subject assignments and show only eligible teachers.

4. Examination Analysis

This is probably the DoS's most important analytical responsibility.

The DoS should be able to analyze:

Overall examination performance.
Performance by grade.
Performance by class.
Performance by subject.
Average marks.
Highest and lowest performing subjects.
Highest and lowest performing classes.
Learners performing exceptionally well.
Learners requiring academic intervention.
Subject performance trends.
Comparison between examinations.
Percentage/pass-rate distributions.
Performance of individual learners.
Areas where learners are consistently struggling.

For example:

School Examination Analysis

Grade 6
────────────────────

Mathematics       58%
English           74%
Science           67%
Kiswahili         71%

Overall Average   67.5%

⚠ Mathematics requires attention

The DoS should be able to drill down from:

School → Grade → Class → Subject → Learner

5. Academic Performance Meetings

The DoS needs the analysis so that during school meetings he can provide insightful points about the academic wellbeing of the school.

So I would give the DoS a dedicated Academic Analysis / Insights section.

It could highlight things like:

Grade 6 Mathematics performance declined by 8% compared with the previous examination.

Mathematics is currently the lowest-performing subject in Grade 6.

14 learners in Grade 5 are below the school's defined performance threshold.

Grade 4A improved by 12% compared with the previous assessment.

This turns the DoS from someone who merely looks at marks into someone who can actually interpret academic data.

6. Curriculum / Teaching Oversight

We also discussed that the DoS can have responsibilities beyond examinations.

I would give him oversight of:

Subject allocation.
Teacher-subject assignments.
Curriculum coverage.
Academic calendars.
Assessment planning.
Monitoring completion of assessments.
Identifying subjects/classes falling behind.
Reviewing teacher academic workload.

However, teacher assignment itself remains primarily under the Deputy based on the structure you gave me.

So I'd distinguish them like this:

Deputy:

"Who teaches what, where, and when?"

DoS:

"How is the academic program and examination process performing?"

That separation will make your permissions much cleaner.

The DoS dashboard

I would therefore structure his dashboard around:

The key distinction I'd preserve in your system is:

Deputy = operational + whole-school academic supervision

DoS = academic/examination administration + deeper academic analysis

That prevents the two roles from becoming unnecessarily duplicated.

18. Official Grading & Assessment Methodology

All examination subjects across all grades are scored out of a maximum of 100 marks.
The institutional grading system distinguishes between learners below Grade 7 and learners in Junior Secondary:

A. Pre-Primary & Primary (Playgroup, PP1, PP2, Grades 1 to 6) — 4-Level CBC Rubrics
Classes from Pre-Primary through Grade 6 follow CBC Competency Rubrics:
- Level 4: E.E (Exceeding Expectations)   -> 75 – 100%
- Level 3: M.E (Meeting Expectations)     -> 50 – 74%
- Level 2: A.E (Approaching Expectations) -> 25 – 49%
- Level 1: B.E (Below Expectations)       -> 0 – 24%

All marksheet entry grids, review queues, student profile modals, and print stationery for PP to Grade 6 strictly display these 4 CBC Rubrics (EE, ME, AE, BE) and do not show Junior Secondary points.

B. Junior Secondary (Grades 7 to 9) — 8-Level Points System
Learners in Junior Secondary follow an 8-level points system:
- 8 points: 90 – 100%
- 7 points: 75 – 89%
- 6 points: 58 – 74%
- 5 points: 41 – 57%
- 4 points: 31 – 40%
- 3 points: 21 – 30%
- 2 points: 11 – 20%
- 1 point:  0 – 10%

All marksheet entry grids, review queues, student profile modals, and print stationery for Junior Secondary strictly display these Points (1 to 8 pts) and do not show Primary rubrics.
